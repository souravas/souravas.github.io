# /start — Keypad

The browser homepage: every bookmark a key, each group a block of keys under its name, the blocks closing into one rectangle sized to the window. [index.html](index.html) holds the bookmarks, [style.css](style.css) the layout, [prompt.js](prompt.js) the prompt, and [main.js](main.js) imports the prompt and registers the service worker.

Keypad is the only design, and there are no `/start/vN` pages. The two it replaced in November 2026 (lists in columns, and *Shelves*) are in the git history, with the `start/bookmarks.html` the three shared.

**Bookmarks**: the groups, the links and their order are in [index.html](index.html); don't copy them into this file. A bookmark is a plain `<li>` link whose icon is a `<symbol>` in the page's sprite (`si-*` Simple Icons, `logo-*` single-colour site logos, `mdi-*` Material Design Icons; 24×24, `currentColor`). Optional attributes:

- `data-alias`: space-separated short codes the prompt matches first.
- `data-search`: a search URL with `%s`; an alias plus a query (`lc two sum`) opens it.
- `data-also`: more URLs, space-separated, opened in new tabs along with the href. Write `data-tabs` beside it, their count plus one for the href, which the key prints; nothing counts it for you.
- `data-opens`: never written by hand. The build (`opener-sites` in vite.config.js, dev too) writes it onto every link to /invest or /cloud: the hosts that page opens, read from its `ol.sites`. The prompt matches them as it does a link's own addresses ("kite" finds Invest), and never opens them itself.

**This site's pages** are keys too: Pomodoro, Fit and Invest in Daily, Python (/code) in Code, Language (/kannada) in Learn, and Downloads, the last key in Fun. Invest and Downloads open [/invest](../invest/CLAUDE.md) and [/downloads](../downloads/CLAUDE.md), each of which opens several sites from a list of its own ([/cloud](../cloud/CLAUDE.md) does the same and has no key: the hidden `ul.links` after the groups holds it for the prompt alone, which finds it by name or by a site it opens, "drive"). /downloads keeps its sites in the browser, so the prompt knows only its name. The Cashflow sheet and the IPO trackers have had no keys of their own since November 2026: Invest opens them, and the prompt finds it by them ("docs", "ipo").

**Groups**: the most used links are the first group, Daily. The groups are Daily's six links and Learn's five, then Code's, Design's and Fun's four, in that order. A group with a fifth link takes the places of six keys, so Learn leaves one place empty, its last, and the groups close into a rectangle around it at every window size. That was the user's choice when the keys went from 32 to 23 in November 2026 (23 keys fill no rectangle). A sixth link in Learn fills the empty place; keep the rest in mind when adding a link or a group (see *Layout*).

**Hooks**: style.css and prompt.js rely on the markup's hooks: `.prompt` with `#q` and `#hint`, and each group a `section.group` holding an `h2` and a `ul.links` of `<a>`s with an `.icon` and a `.name`. The hidden `ul.links` after the groups holds pages with no key; its links need only a `.name`. The `h1` (the page's title, Hope) is for screen readers only. Keep those when changing the markup.

- **Legends**: a key prints its short code (`data-alias`) top left and, when it opens several tabs, their count top right, from `data-tabs`. Both are CSS `content` with empty alt text, so screen readers read only the name.
- **Layout**: one grid of key columns (`--cols`). A group takes as many columns as it sets keys to a row: `--six` for a group of six links (any group with a fifth link: Daily, Learn), `--four` for the rest. The arrangement follows the window's shape:

  | Window | Columns | Rows of groups |
  |---|---|---|
  | under 360px | 2 | each group a row of its own, two keys wide |
  | phones, and from 760px windows narrower than 4:5 | 4 | Daily Learn (two keys wide, three deep) / Code Design (two by two) / Fun (one row of four) |
  | from 760px and 4:5 or wider | 6 | Daily Learn (three keys wide, two deep) / Code Design Fun (two by two) |
  | from 1160px and 7:3 or wider | 12 | all five in one row, shaped as in six columns |

  **The arrangements count on Daily's six and Learn's five, then three groups of four** (24 places with Learn's last empty: 2 × 12, 4 × 6, 6 × 4, 12 × 2). Any other link added or removed, or another group, leaves holes, so look at every arrangement after changing the groups. The ratios are where the next arrangement starts to give larger keys.

  In two and four columns every key is one width and sits under the key above it. Four columns leave Fun alone in its row of groups, so it is one row of four, with a spacer track between its second and third keys that splits it into two pairs under the pairs above; the rule finds it as `.group:last-of-type`, so Fun has to stay the last group. In six and twelve columns a group of six is three keys across where a group of four is two, so its keys are a fortieth of a key wider (4px at 2560 × 945) and line up with the others only at the groups' edges.
- **Fit**: from 760px the page is sized to the window and centred. `--key`, a key's height, is the largest at which the arrangement fits both ways, up to 160px, and the gaps, icons and type all follow it. The formula's numbers come from the gap ratios, the tallest the prompt and a heading get (47px and 24px), and `--across`, how many keys wide the page is while its narrowest keys are square, gaps and padding included (0.35 + 1.175 × `--cols` in every arrangement, so style.css computes it: 5.05, 7.4, 14.45), as the comment above it in style.css spells out: change one and the other has to follow, or the page stops fitting. A key is between square and a quarter wider than tall. On a maximised window the page fills about three fifths of the area: 2560 × 945px gives twelve columns of 181 × 160px keys (the cap; the page takes the window's full width), 1536 × 725px six of 141 × 113px.
- **Smallest**: a key goes down to 80px, the least that holds a name on two lines (the second runs into the key's lower padding); names wrap in keys under about 97px wide. Twelve columns start at 1160px, where their keys are 80px wide, and six reach 80px only in windows under about 540px tall; half a laptop's screen (768 × 725px) gets six columns of 104px keys. In four columns from 760px, only in tall windows where keys are wide, it goes to 72px. "AlgoMonster", the longest word, needs a key about 76px wide with the key's 2px side padding; from 760px every key is wider.

**Prompt**: the header comment in [prompt.js](prompt.js) describes how the prompt matches and ranks. Links open in the same tab because the page is the browser homepage. Set as a browser search engine, `https://souravas.com/start/?q=%s` takes the prompt's queries from the address bar: the page replaces itself with what Enter would open, or, when that opens several tabs (which needs a click or key press), leaves the query in the prompt. **An address in `?q=` also waits for Enter, never opening by itself**: otherwise anyone could write a souravas.com link that lands on a page of theirs. Only the page's own bookmarks, their searches and Google open unasked. Clicking a group heading (a button injected by `prompt.js`) opens the whole group in new tabs; browsers block every tab after the first until the site is allowed pop-ups.

**Offline**: [public/sw.js](../public/sw.js), the service worker the personal pages share, is registered in production by main.js with the scope `/start`. For this page it serves the page from cache and refreshes the cache in the background, so a deploy shows up on the second open. A browser that opened one of the old designs at /start/v1, /start/v2 or /start/v3 (now 404s) keeps that design's worker and cache, and goes on serving it at that address until the worker is unregistered (`chrome://serviceworker-internals` or the browser's site settings); /start itself is unaffected.
