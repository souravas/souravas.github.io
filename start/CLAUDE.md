# /start — Keypad

The browser homepage: every bookmark a key, each group a block of keys under its name, the blocks closing into one rectangle sized to the window. [index.html](index.html) holds the bookmarks, [style.css](style.css) the layout, [prompt.js](prompt.js) the prompt, and [main.js](main.js) imports the prompt and registers the service worker.

Keypad is the only design, and there are no `/start/vN` pages. The two it replaced in November 2026 (lists in columns, and *Shelves*) are in the git history, with the `start/bookmarks.html` the three shared.

**Bookmarks**: the groups, the links and their order are in [index.html](index.html); don't copy them into this file. A bookmark is a plain `<li>` link whose icon is a `<symbol>` in the page's sprite (`si-*` Simple Icons, `logo-*` single-colour site logos, `mdi-*` Material Design Icons; 24×24, `currentColor`). Optional attributes:

- `data-alias`: space-separated short codes the prompt matches first.
- `data-search`: a search URL with `%s`; an alias plus a query (`lc two sum`) opens it.
- `data-also`: more URLs, space-separated, opened in new tabs along with the href. Write `data-tabs` beside it, their count plus one for the href, which the key prints; nothing counts it for you.
- `data-opens`: never written by hand. The build (`opener-sites` in vite.config.js, dev too) writes it onto every link to /invest or /cloud: the hosts that page opens, read from its `ol.sites`. The prompt matches them as it does a link's own addresses ("kite" finds Invest), and never opens them itself.
- `data-hello-interview`: the first activation of this key or its prompt result asks for an HTTP or HTTPS URL. `prompt.js` stores it in `localStorage` as `start:hello-interview` and opens it immediately. Like Downloads, the address stays in that browser. The prompt matches the key by name and alias, and opening all of Hope skips it until it has a saved destination.

**This site's pages** are keys too: Fit and Pomodoro in Daily, Code (/code) in Hope, Language (/kannada) in Learn, and Invest and Downloads, the last two keys in Misc. Invest and Downloads open [/invest](../invest/CLAUDE.md) and [/downloads](../downloads/CLAUDE.md), each of which opens several sites from a list of its own ([/cloud](../cloud/CLAUDE.md) does the same and has no key: the hidden `ul.links` after the groups holds it for the prompt alone, which finds it by name or by a site it opens, "drive"). /downloads keeps its sites in the browser, so the prompt knows only its name. The Cashflow sheet and the IPO trackers have had no keys of their own since November 2026: Invest opens them, and the prompt finds it by them ("docs", "ipo").

**Groups**: the most used links are the first group, Daily, pinned to the top. The groups are Daily's four links, Hope's eight (the interview-prep sites, Code and System Design), Learn's three and Misc's six, in that order. Learn keeps the same width as Misc so they can share a row in the four- and six-column layouts.

**Hooks**: style.css and prompt.js rely on the markup's hooks: `.prompt` with `#q` and `#hint`, and each group a `section.group` holding an `h2` and a `ul.links` of `<a>`s with an `.icon` and a `.name`. The hidden `ul.links` after the groups holds pages with no key; its links need only a `.name`. The `h1` (the page's title, Hope) is for screen readers only. Keep those when changing the markup.

- **Legends**: a key prints its short code (`data-alias`) top left and, when it opens several tabs, their count top right, from `data-tabs`. Both are CSS `content` with empty alt text, so screen readers read only the name.
- **Layout**: one grid of key columns (`--cols`). A group takes as many columns as it sets keys to a row: `--four` for a group of up to four links (Daily), `--six` for Learn or a group with a fifth link (Misc), `--eight` for one with a seventh (Hope). The arrangement follows the window's shape:

  | Window | Columns | Rows of groups |
  |---|---|---|
  | under 360px | 2 | each group a row of its own, two keys wide |
  | phones, and from 760px windows narrower than 4:5 | 4 | Daily (one row of four) / Hope (two rows of four) / Learn Misc (two keys wide, up to three rows) |
  | from 760px and 4:5 or wider | 6 | Daily (two by two) Hope (four by two) / Learn (three by one) Misc (three by two) |
  | from 1160px: under 543px tall, or 3:1 or wider and under 880px tall | 12 | all four in one row, shaped as in six columns |

  **The arrangements count on the widths of Daily, Hope, Learn and Misc**, so look at every arrangement after changing the groups. 4:5 is where six columns start to give larger keys. **Twelve columns are kept for short windows**, the user's choice in November 2026: they used to start at 7:3, where their keys first came out taller, and on the user's 21:9 screen they stretched the page into one band from edge to edge with most of the height empty. Now they take over only where six columns would scroll (under 543px tall) or where their keys come out at least a fifth larger in area (3:1 and under 880px tall); the comment above the rule in style.css works it through.

  In two and four columns every key is one width and sits under the key above it. Four columns set Daily and Hope four keys across, each alone in its row of groups, with a spacer track between the second and third keys of every row that splits them into pairs over Learn's and Misc's. In six and twelve columns Daily is two keys across, Learn and Misc three and Hope four, so their keys are a fortieth and three eightieths of a key wider than Daily's (4px and 6px at 2560 × 945) and line up with the others only at the groups' edges.
- **Fit**: from 760px the page is sized to the window and centred. `--key`, a key's height, is the largest at which the arrangement fits both ways, up to 160px, and the gaps, icons and type all follow it. The formula's numbers come from the gap ratios, the tallest the prompt and a heading get (47px and 24px), and `--across`, how many keys wide the page is while its narrowest keys are square, gaps and padding included (0.35 + 1.175 × `--cols` in every arrangement, so style.css computes it: 5.05, 7.4, 14.45), as the comment above it in style.css spells out: change one and the other has to follow, or the page stops fitting. A key is between square and a quarter wider than tall. On a maximised window the page fills the window's height and half to two thirds of its area: 2560 × 945px gives six columns of 190 × 152px keys (twelve would give 181 × 160px, the cap, across the window's full width), 1536 × 725px six of 141 × 113px.
- **Smallest**: a key goes down to 80px, the least that holds a name on two lines (the second runs into the key's lower padding); names wrap in keys under about 97px wide. Twelve columns start at 1160px, where their keys are 80px wide, and six reach 80px only in windows under about 540px tall; half a laptop's screen (768 × 725px) gets six columns of 104px keys. In four columns from 760px, only in tall windows where keys are wide, it goes to 72px. "Downloads", the longest word since Algo Monster got its space, needs a key about 67px wide with the key's 2px side padding; from 760px every key is wider.

**Prompt**: the header comment in [prompt.js](prompt.js) describes how the prompt matches and ranks. Links open in the same tab because the page is the browser homepage. Set as a browser search engine, `https://souravas.com/start/?q=%s` takes the prompt's queries from the address bar: the page replaces itself with what Enter would open, or, when that opens several tabs (which needs a click or key press), leaves the query in the prompt. **An address in `?q=` also waits for Enter, never opening by itself**: otherwise anyone could write a souravas.com link that lands on a page of theirs. Only the page's own bookmarks, their searches and Google open unasked. Clicking a group heading (a button injected by `prompt.js`) opens the whole group in new tabs; browsers block every tab after the first until the site is allowed pop-ups.

**Offline**: [public/sw.js](../public/sw.js), the service worker the personal pages share, is registered in production by main.js with the scope `/start`. For this page it serves the page from cache and refreshes the cache in the background, so a deploy shows up on the second open. A browser that opened one of the old designs at /start/v1, /start/v2 or /start/v3 (now 404s) keeps that design's worker and cache, and goes on serving it at that address until the worker is unregistered (`chrome://serviceworker-internals` or the browser's site settings); /start itself is unaffected.
