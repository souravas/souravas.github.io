Reading the spelling

Kannada is written here in English letters. Every Kannada line starts lowercase, so a capital letter is always a sound, never the start of a sentence. Names keep their capitals.

  1. A capital T, D, N or L: curl the tongue back to touch the roof of the mouth. haaLu is ruined, haalu is milk.
  2. A capital E, I or O, or a doubled aa, ee or oo: hold the vowel twice as long, as in bEku, want, and neeru, water.
  3. A doubled consonant is held long too: katte is a donkey, kate a story.

A hyphen only shows where an ending joins a word: maaD-taa is said as one word, maaDtaa. The note's Notation has the whole of it.

How a day goes

Review what is due, read the lesson out loud, take its quiz, listen to five minutes of real Kannada, and say one line to someone. Done, at the end of the lesson, moves you on, and the next lesson waits for tomorrow. Cover, in the bar, hides the Kannada so you can say it from the English. Lessons opens any lesson, and Whole note has everything.
